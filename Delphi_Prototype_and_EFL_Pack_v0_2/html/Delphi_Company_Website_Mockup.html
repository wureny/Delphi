The missing layer

Investment teams do not need another place to ask questions.

They need a reliable way to maintain what the firm believes as facts, forecasts and risks change.

≠

Summaries stop at the document

A good summary can explain what a filing said. It usually does not know which approved assumption changed—or whether the team accepted that change.

⌁

Decisions disappear into tools

Thesis changes are scattered across spreadsheets, chat, email and meetings, making later reconstruction slow and unreliable.

!

Automation without boundaries erodes trust

Unverified model output should not silently rewrite formal views, forecasts or portfolio workflows.

Questions

What Delphi is—and is not.

Is Delphi another financial chatbot?

No. Chat can be an auxiliary interface, but the primary object is a reviewable change against approved investment state. The core workflow ends in a governed Decision Ledger, not a conversation transcript.

Does Delphi replace Bloomberg, FactSet, AlphaSense, Excel or a PMS?

The initial product is an improvement layer across existing data, models and collaboration tools. It targets the manual connections, thesis trackers and decision-history gaps between them.

Does the model make investment decisions?

No. Model outputs are proposals with evidence, confidence, uncertainty, materiality and required reviewer. Authorized people decide what becomes formal state.

Does Delphi promise investment performance?

No. The initial value proposition is research operations, evidence quality, attention allocation, governance and institutional memory—not guaranteed alpha or automated trading.

Is the customer-owned deployment already production-ready?

It is the intended institutional architecture. The current public prototype demonstrates the contracts, policy surfaces and data-boundary design using synthetic fixtures; production deployment must be validated separately.

Delphi is the fund’s harness—not the model’s harness.
