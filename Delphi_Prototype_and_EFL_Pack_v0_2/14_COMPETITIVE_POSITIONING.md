# Delphi Competitive Positioning v0.2

**Updated:** July 13, 2026  
**Purpose:** credible positioning, not a claim that competitors lack modern AI capabilities

---

## 1. Competitive reality

By 2026, leading finance AI platforms publicly describe capabilities that include:

- private and public data integration;
- finance-specific context;
- agents and end-to-end workflows;
- structured and unstructured data;
- citations and traceability;
- enterprise security;
- institutional knowledge;
- multi-agent research and report generation.

Therefore, Delphi must not position itself as unique merely because it has:

```text
RAG
multi-agent execution
citations
private documents
financial context
workflow automation
reports and slides
audit language
```

These are category requirements.

## 2. Competitor categories

| Category | Examples | Center of gravity | Delphi response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market intelligence / search | AlphaSense | premium content, search, deep research, integrated research workflows | integrate or coexist; own approved state and transition |
| Institutional AI workspace | Hebbia | shared context, private data, scalable finance workflows, institutional knowledge | differentiate on canonical investment state and approval semantics |
| Finance-specific agent platform | Rogo | agents embedded in firm systems and end-to-end finance work | narrow to public-markets thesis maintenance and governed state |
| AI-native finance workflow | Finster | data synthesis, agents, traceable outputs, proactive workflows, private deployment | do not compete on generic orchestration; own Decision Ledger and materiality |
| General-purpose model stack | OpenAI / Anthropic / open models + frameworks | rapidly improving reasoning, search, coding, and agents | remain model-agnostic and capture durable institutional context |
| Internal build | fund engineering / analyst scripts | customer-specific fit and control | sell production responsibility and repeatable workflow assets |
| Manual status quo | Excel, Drive, Slack, email | familiar, flexible, low direct cost | prove lower coordination cost and better traceability without forcing replacement |

## 3. Current public competitor evidence

### Hebbia

Public positioning includes “AI for Finance,” “Institutional Intelligence,” finance-specific automated workflows, shared context becoming institutional knowledge, and connection of private documents, public filings, and financial data providers.

**Implication:** Delphi cannot claim that institutional knowledge or private-data workflows are absent. It must define the canonical state and approval object more precisely.

### Rogo

Rogo publicly describes itself as an AI partner to leading financial institutions, with finance-specific agents that understand and execute workflows and integrate with firm systems and financial data.

**Implication:** “Finance-specific agents embedded in systems” is not unique.

### AlphaSense

AlphaSense publicly positions a broad AI market-intelligence platform with hundreds of millions of documents, integrated workflows, multi-agent architecture, customizable agents, financial data, citations, and auditability.

**Implication:** Delphi should not compete as another broad research/search platform. It should coexist and consume outputs while governing firm-specific state.

### Finster

Finster publicly describes enterprise AI for finance, proactive agent workflows, proprietary data, traceable insights, citations, model orchestration, integrations, asset-management coverage, and private deployment.

**Implication:** Delphi cannot claim source traceability, model independence, proactive agents, or private deployment direction as uniquely sufficient.

## 4. Delphi’s credible wedge

> **Delphi is the governed state-transition layer for a fund’s approved investment view.**

### Product center

```text
Competitor center:
find → synthesize → generate → automate

Delphi center:
compare against approved state
→ propose typed change
→ validate
→ authorize
→ record point-in-time transition
→ evaluate later
```

### Objects Delphi must make first-class

- approved thesis version;
- assumption / forecast / risk / open question;
- evidence relation;
- materiality policy;
- required reviewer;
- human edit and rationale;
- state-transition gateway;
- before/after hash;
- Decision Ledger;
- outcome and postmortem;
- firm-specific eval.

If these are merely labels around a report, the differentiation is not real.

## 6. Claims to avoid

Do not say:

- “Competitors only summarize documents.”
- “No other platform has agents.”
- “No one integrates private data.”
- “AlphaSense is just search.”
- “Hebbia has no institutional memory.”
- “Rogo cannot execute workflows.”
- “Finster has no audit trail.”
- “Delphi eliminates hallucinations.”
- “Our graph is the moat.”
- “Multi-agent is our moat.”
- “Customer VPC makes us unique.”
- “We are the first AI for finance.”

These are likely false, fragile, or too broad.

## 7. Proof needed for differentiation

### Product proof

- formal state exists before a run;
- proposals point to exact target objects;
- approvals are role-aware;
- accepted edits create a new version;
- rejected items do not mutate;
- ledger can replay;
- outcomes can link back to decisions.

### Commercial proof

- customers care about thesis maintenance;
- they will expose or map approved state;
- they value Decision Ledger enough to pay;
- PM attention precision improves;
- pilot embeds in recurring review;
- second workflow expands naturally.

### Technical proof

- state contracts survive model replacement;
- point-in-time replay prevents hindsight leakage;
- materiality eval is firm-specific;
- customer-owned deployment preserves useful functionality;
- no customer-specific code fork is required.

## 8. Competitive scenarios

### If AlphaSense / Hebbia adds explicit thesis objects

Delphi must win on depth of state semantics, approval, evaluation, customer-owned deployment, workflow specificity, and faster implementation for the target ICP. If the gap collapses, prioritize partnerships or a more specialized strategy pack.

### If a frontier model handles the full analysis

Use it. Delphi should become better, because the model still needs:

- permitted context;
- current approved state;
- tool and data policy;
- transition validation;
- human authorization;
- versioning;
- institutional memory;
- outcome evaluation.

### If customers prefer internal builds

Offer:

- open interfaces;
- customer-owned data plane;
- workflow and evaluation assets;
- implementation playbook;
- maintenance responsibility;
- exit and export path.

The product should be easier to adopt than recreating production infrastructure while preserving customer control.

---

## 9. Internal competitive test

Ask quarterly:

1. If model reasoning becomes ten times better, is Delphi more valuable?
2. If the customer switches models, does the product retain its value?
3. If a competitor copies the UI, what data, workflow, and trust assets remain?
4. Does every customer use the same core object and transition model?
5. Are users approving state changes, or merely consuming generated reports?
6. Can the customer explain why Delphi is different without saying “better AI”?

If the answer to the last question is no, positioning is not yet defensible.
